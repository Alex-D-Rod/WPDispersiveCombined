Introduction

The equation and a brief history

\[ \begin{cases} i\partial_tu+\partial_x^2u+\mathcal N(u)u=0,\\ u(x,0)=u_0(x), \end{cases} \qquad \mathcal N(u)=\sum_k d_k\abs u^{\alpha_k}. \]
  • LWP/GWP in a Sobolev space for single and two nonlinear terms (Ohta '95, Tao-Visan-Zhang '07, Pocovnicu et al. '12, Carles-Sparber '21, ...)
  • LWP/GWP in weighted Sobolev spaces Casenave-Naumkin '16 allowing for small power nonlinearity in any dimension
Question: Can we construct a weighted-space not only treats small powers, but also many or infinitely many powers at once?
The Nonlinear term

Why small powers fail and the solution

The regularity issue

For small nonlinear terms \(0 < \alpha < 1\) differentiating \(|u|^\alpha u\) can introduce singularities.

\[ \partial (\abs u^{\alpha}) \approx \alpha \abs u^{\alpha-1} \abs \partial u. \]

If u becomes 0, the term becomes singular.

The solution

If we ensure that u is bounded away from zero, the term remains well-behaved.

\[ \inf_{x\in \mathbb{R}} \la x\ra^n \abs{u_0(x)} \geq \lambda > 0. \]

This ensures that the initial data is bounded away from zero, but how do we maintain this bound as time evolves?

Using the FTC and the Sobolev embedding then

\[ \inf_{x\in \mathbb{R}} \la x\ra^n \abs{u(t,x)} \geq \inf_{x\in \mathbb{R}} \la x\ra^n \abs{u_0(x)} - t \norm{\la x\ra^n \partial_x^2 u}_{L^\infty} \geq \lambda - t\norm{\la x\ra^n \partial_x^2 u}_{H^1}. \]

Let \(u = e^{it\partial_x^2}u_0\), then if we control \(\norm{\la x\ra^n e^{it\partial_x^2}\partial_x^2 u_0}_{H^1}\) we can maintain the lower bound for finite time.

Controlling the weighted linear flow

Two different approaches

Casenave-Naumkin method:

Use integer weights and an induction to show

\[ \norm{\la x \ra^n e^{it\partial_x^2}\partial_x^\beta u_0(x)}_{L^2} \leq \sum_{k=0}^\beta \norm{\la x \ra^{n-k} \partial_x^k u_0(x)}_{L^2}. \]

A variation:

Use a Nahas-Ponce interpolation to show

\[ \|\langle x \rangle^{b}e^{it\partial_x^2}f\|_{L^2} \leq C \langle t \rangle^b\big( \|J^{b}f\|_{L^2}+\|\langle x\rangle^b f\|_{L^2} \big). \]

This avoids the induction and allows for \(b \in \R^+\)

The benifits of 1D:

In 1D the weighted linear flow can be controlled directly via the Sobolev embedding and bound above, with no need to take extra derivatives in \(L^\infty\).

The challenges of higher dimensions:

In higher dimensions, the control of the linear flow becomes significantly more complex, requiring the use of a Taylor expansion

\[ e^{it\Delta}f= \sum^P_{j = 0}\frac{(it)^j}{j!}\Delta^jf + \frac{i^{P+1}}{P!}\int_0^t(t-s)^P e^{is\Delta}\Delta^{P+1}f(s)ds. \]
Deciding where our solution lives

Construction of the weighted space \(\mathcal{X}\)

The computations on the linear flow lead to the construction of the weighted space.

\[ \X=\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} &u\in\dot H^M(\R)\cap\dot H^1(\R):\\ \mathrm{I.}&\la x\ra^n u\in L^\infty,\\ \mathrm{II.}&\la x\ra^n\partial_x^\beta u\in L^2, \quad 1\leq\beta\leq r,\\ \mathrm{III.}&\partial_x^\beta u\in L^2, \quad r+1\leq\beta\leq M \end{array}\right. \]
\({n > 0}, \; r\geq 3, \, \, \text{ and }\, \, M \geq n+ r.\)
\[\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}} = \|\langle x \rangle^n u\|_{L^{\infty}} + \sum_{k=1}^{r} \|\langle x \rangle^{n} \partial_{x}^{k} u\|_{L^2} + {\sum_{k=r+1}^{M} \|\partial_{x}^{k} u\|_{L^2}}\]

What is different here?

We take fractional power weights, leverage the flexibility of the 1D setting to avoid derivatives in \(L^\infty\), and remove depedence on \(\alpha\) in the space.

Note: We do not require \(u \in L^2\) for this construction, but for \(n > \frac{1}{2}\) the space \(\mathcal{X}\) is embedded into \(H^M(\mathbb{R})\)

Controlling the nonlinear term

Nonlinear estimates

We now focus on the \(B\) term

\[ \Big\| {\langle x \rangle^n \sum_{\substack{\gamma + \rho = \beta \\ \gamma \neq 0}}\sum_{k = 1}^{\gamma}\sum_{\substack{l_1+...+l_k=\gamma, \\ l_j \geq 1}} \tilde C_{l_1...l_k}\Big(|u|^{\alpha - 2k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\partial_x^{l_j}(|u|^2) \Big)\partial_x^\rho u} \Big\|_{L^2} \]
\[ \sum_{\substack{\gamma + \rho = \beta \\ \gamma \neq 0}}\sum_{k = 1}^{\gamma}\sum_{\substack{l_1+...+l_k=\gamma, \\ l_j \geq 1}}\sum_{\substack{ 0\leq m_j\leq \ell_j \\ j=1,\dots, k}} \tilde C_{l_1...l_k}\Big\| {\langle x \rangle^n |u|^{\alpha - 2k}\partial_{x}^{m_1}u\partial_{x}^{\ell_{1}-m_1}u...\partial_x^{m_{k}}u\partial_{x}^{\ell_{k}-m_{k}}u \partial_x^{\rho}u} \Big\|_{L^2} \]
\[ \Big\| {\langle x \rangle^n |u|^{\alpha - 2k}\partial_{x}^{m_1}u\partial_{x}^{\ell_{1}-m_1}u...\partial_x^{m_{k}}u\partial_{x}^{\ell_{k}-m_{k}}u \partial_x^{\rho}u} \Big\|_{L^2} \]
\[ \Big\| {\underline{\langle x \rangle^n |u|^{\alpha - 2k}}\partial_{x}^{m_1}u\partial_{x}^{\ell_{1}-m_1}u...\partial_x^{m_{k}}u\partial_{x}^{\ell_{k}-m_{k}}u \partial_x^{\rho}u} \Big\|_{L^2} \]
\[ \big(\lambda^{-|\alpha-2k|}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{|\alpha-2k|}\big)\Big\| {\la x \ra^{n-n\alpha + 2kn}\partial_{x}^{m_1}u\partial_{x}^{\ell_{1}-m_1}u...\partial_x^{m_{k}}u\partial_{x}^{\ell_{k}-m_{k}}u \partial_x^{\rho}u} \Big\|_{L^2} \]
\[ \big(\lambda^{-|\alpha-2k|}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{|\alpha-2k|}\big)\Big\| {\la x \ra^{-n\alpha}\la x \ra^{n}\partial_{x}^{m_1}\la x \ra^{n}u\partial_{x}^{\ell_{1}-m_1}u...\la x \ra^{n}\partial_x^{m_{k}}u\la x \ra^{n}\partial_{x}^{\ell_{k}-m_{k}}u \la x \ra^{n}\partial_x^{\rho}u} \Big\|_{L^2} \]
\[ \big(\lambda^{-|\alpha-2k|}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{|\alpha-2k|}\big)\| {\la x \ra^{-n\alpha}\|_{L^\infty}\|\la x \ra^{n}\partial_x^{\rho}u}\|_{L^2}\prod_{j=1}^k\|\la x \ra^{n}\partial_{x}^{m_j}\|_{L^\infty}\|\la x \ra^{n}u\partial_{x}^{\ell_{j}-m_j}u\|_{L^\infty} \]
\[ \big(\lambda^{-|\alpha-2k|}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{|\alpha-2k|}\big) \|u\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2k + 1} \]

We choose \(\rho\) to be the largest order derivative without loss of generality, but note that this is not necessary.

Re-introducing the sums and a few upper bounds, the final estimate for \(B\) is

\[ B \lesssim \sum_{\substack{\gamma + \rho = \beta \\ \gamma \neq 0}}\sum_{k = 1}^{\gamma}\sum_{\substack{l_1+...+l_k=\gamma, \\ l_j \geq 1}}\big(\lambda^{-|\alpha-2k|}+\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{|\alpha-2k|}\big)\sum_{\substack{ 0\leq m_j\leq \ell_j \\ j=1,\dots, k}} \big((n+1)C\big)^{2k}\tbinom{\ell_1}{m_1}\dots \tbinom{\ell_{k}}{m_{k}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2k+1} \]

The main takeaway here is that we keep the leftover weight \( \la x\ra^{- n \alpha} \) in \(L^\infty\), removing the dependance on \(\alpha\) in \(\mathcal{X}\).

Taking advantage of our new freedom

Summing over many powers

With our space free of the dependence in \(\alpha\), we can now sum over finitely or infinitely many powers, under one condition.

Condition \((\ast)\):

\[ \sum_{k=0}^{N}\sum_{0\leq \beta \leq M}|d_k|\big(1+C(\alpha_k,\beta))(R_0^{|\alpha_k-2\beta|}+|\alpha_k-2\beta|R_0^{|\alpha_k-2\beta-1|})<\infty \]

for any \(R_0>0\).

For \(N\) finite this is straightforward, but what if \(N\to\infty\)?

Examples of nonlinearities

What satisfies our condition?

The answers are quite interesting!

Finite sums: \(\;\mathcal{N}(u) = c_1|u|^{\alpha_1} + c_2|u|^{\alpha_2} ... + c_N|u|^{\alpha_N}\)
Trigonometric Functions: \(\mathcal{N}(u) = \sin(\alpha|u|^\gamma)\;\) or \(\;\mathcal{N}(u) = \cos(\alpha|u|^\gamma)\)

Exponential Functions:

\(\qquad \mathcal{N}(u) = e^{\alpha|u|^\gamma}\)
\(\mathcal{X}\)
\(\pm|u|^{p}u \pm |u|^{q}u\)
\(\cos(\alpha|u|^\gamma)u\)
\(\sin(\alpha|u|^\gamma)u\)
\(e^{\alpha|u|^\gamma}u\)
\(\pm|u|^{\alpha_1}u \pm |u|^{\alpha_2}u \pm |u|^{\alpha_3}u + ...\)

LWP Theorem

(Riaño, ADR, Roudenko) Nonlinearity '26

Let \(\{d_k\}\) be a sequence of complex numbers and \(\{\alpha_k\}\) be a sequence of positive real numbers. Suppose that condition \((\ast)\) holds. If \(u_0 \in \mathcal{X}\) satisfies non-vanishing condition, then there exist \(T>0\) and a unique solution \(u \in C([-T,T], \mathcal{X})\) of the combined NLS such that

\[ \sup_{t\in [-T,T]}\|\langle x\rangle^n(u(t)-u_0)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb R)}\leq \frac{\lambda}{2}. \]
Moreover, the map \(u_0 \mapsto u(\cdot,t)\) is Lipshitz continuous.

From a local to a global solution

LWP for the non-autonomous problem on \(0\leq\tau\leq b^{-1}\):

\[ iv_\tau+v_{xx}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}d_k(1-b\tau)^{\alpha_k/2-2}|v|^{\alpha_k}v=0. \]

The coefficient is integrable exactly when \(\alpha_k>2\):

\[ \int_0^{1/b}(1-b\tau)^{\alpha_k/2-2}\,d\tau =\frac{2}{b(\alpha_k-2)}. \]

global theorem

Global well-posedness and scattering

Theorem (Riaño–Rodriguez–Roudenko '26):
Assume \(n>1/2\), \(\alpha_k>2\) for every \(k\).

\[ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{0\leq \beta \leq M}\frac{|d_k|}{\alpha_k-2}\big(1+C(\alpha_k,\beta))(R_0^{|\alpha_k-2\beta|}+|\alpha_k-2\beta|R_0^{|\alpha_k-2\beta-1|})<\infty. \]

Let \(v_0\in\X\) satisfy the non-vanishing condition and set \(u_0=e^{ibx^2/4}v_0\). For \(b>0\) sufficiently large, there is a unique global solution \(u(x,t)\) with

\[ u_0\in C([0,\infty);H^{s_n}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{\infty}\big([0,\infty);L^{\infty}(\langle x \rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\, dx)\big), \] \[ s_n=1\quad(n>3/2), \qquad 0< s_n < n-1/2\quad(1/2 < n \leq 3/2). \]

Moreover, there exists \(u_+\in H^{s_n}\) such that

\[ \norm{u(t)-e^{it\partial_x^2}u_+}_{H^{s_n}}\to0, \qquad \sup_{t\geq0}(1+t)^{1/2}\norm{u(t)}_{L^\infty}\lt\infty. \]

higher dimensions

Candidate spaces and new obstacles

\(2D\) candidate

\[ \X_{2D}=\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} &u\in H^M(\R^2):\\ &\la x\ra^b u\in L^\infty,\\ &\la x\ra^{b+1}\partial^\beta u\in L^\infty,\quad 1\leq|\beta|\leq2,\\ &\la x\ra^{b+1}\partial^\beta u\in L^2,\quad 3\leq|\beta|\leq r,\\ &\partial^\beta u\in L^2, \quad r+1 \leq|\beta|\leq M. \end{array}\right. \]

Why it gets difficult

  • Space construction depends on the Sobolev embedding.
  • Several weighted \(L^\infty\) levels must be inserted.
  • The linear and nonlinear estimates ask for different weight requirements.

The balancing act is to keep the space \(\alpha\)-independent.

\(3D\) candidate

\[ \X_{3D}=\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} &u\in H^M(\R^3):\\ &\la x\ra^b u\in L^\infty,\\ &\la x\ra^{b+1}\partial u\in L^\infty,\\ &\la x\ra^{b+3/2}\partial^2u\in L^\infty,\\ &\la x\ra^{b+1}\partial^3u\in L^2,\\ &\la x\ra^{b+3/2}\partial^\beta u\in L^2, \quad 4\leq|\beta|\leq r,\\ &\partial^\beta u\in L^2, \quad r+1 \leq|\beta|\leq M. \end{array}\right. \]

The nonlinear Klein–Gordon

The same architecture for the NKG

\[ w_{tt}-w_{xx}+\mu_1 w+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\nu_k|w|^{\alpha_k}w=0. \]

Set \(\langle |\nabla|\rangle=(1-\partial_x^2)^{1/2}\) and

\[ U=\frac12\left(\mathbf a w+i\mathbf b\langle |\nabla|\rangle^{-1}w_t\right), \qquad A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}. \]

Then KG becomes a first-order half-wave system:

\[ iU_t-A \langle |\nabla|\rangle U-\mathcal M(U)- \sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathcal N_k(U)=0. \]

\(\mathcal{M}(U) = \frac{\mu_1 - 1}{2}[\langle |\nabla|\rangle^{-1}({\bf a}\cdot U)]{\bf b}\) and \(\mathcal{N}_k(U) = -\frac{\nu_{k}}{2}[\langle |\nabla|\rangle^{-1}|{\bf a}\cdot U|^{\alpha_k} ({\bf a}\cdot U)]{\bf b}\).

\[ \mathcal{X}_d=\left\{\begin{aligned} & u\in H^{\frac{3}{2}+}(\mathbb{R},\C^d):\\ &\langle x\rangle^n u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\C^d), \\ &\langle x\rangle^n \langle |\nabla|\rangle^{\frac{3}{2}+}u\in L^2(\mathbb{R},\C^d), \end{aligned}\right. \]
The trick here is using pseudo-differential operators and the same fractional weight tricks from the NLS case on the transformed half-wave system.
We can then reverse the transformation and obtain LWP in a weak sense.

Background

Blow-up via the virial identity

Single power

\[ V[u](t)=\int_{\R}x^2|u(x,t)|^2\,dx, \] \[ V_{tt}=2(5-p)\norm{u_x}_{L^2}^2+4(p-1)E[u]. \]

Case 1: \(p\geq5\), \(E\lt0\): blow-up is direct.

(Glassey '77, Vlasov-Petrishev-Talanov '71)

Case 2: \(p>5\), \(E>0\): more work is needed.

(Energy-critical: Kenig-Merle '06, Intercritical: Holmer-Roudenko '07, Lushikov '95, Holmer-Platte-Roudenko '10)

negative energy

Exponential nonlinearity

Energy

\[ E[u]=\frac12\norm{u_x}_{L^2}^2 -\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+2)k!}\|u\|_{L^{k+2}}^{k+2}. \]

Virial identity

\[ V_{tt}[u] =8\norm{u_x}_{L^2}^2 -4\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{k}{(k+2)k!}\|u\|_{L^{k+2}}^{k+2}. \]

Theorem: exponential blow-up. Let \(u_0\in H^1(\R)\) have finite variance. If

\[E[u_0]+\kappa M[u_0]\lt0,\qquad \kappa\approx14.797,\]

then the solution of \(iu_t+u_{xx}+e^{|u|}u=0\) blows up in finite positive time.

positive energy

Two nonlinear terms

\[ \begin{cases} iu_t + u_{xx} - |\epsilon_1||u|^{4}u + |\epsilon_2||u|^{q-1}u = 0 \\ u(x,0) = u_0. \end{cases} \]

Virial identity

\[ V_{tt}[u] = 2\left[5- q\right]\| u_x\|_{L^2}^2 + \frac{2|\epsilon_1|}{3}\left[5-q\right]\|u\|_{L^{6}}^{6} +4(q-1)E[u]. \]

Blow-up criterion
